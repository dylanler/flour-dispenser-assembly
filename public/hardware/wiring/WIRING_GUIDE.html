Use the BOJACK 400-point breadboard as four separate junctions. Mount the ESP32 separately on the left outside wall and the breadboard on the right outside wall. The scale has no electrical connection.

Unplug the computer USB and the 5V brick before inserting or moving jumpers. Header panels below show logical labels, not verified physical pin order. Match the markings on your actual ESP32, motor module and adapter. GPIO18 means the pin labelled 18 / IO18 / D18, not the eighteenth pin in a header.

Computer → ESP32 only

MacBook → USB-C data cable → ESP32 USB-C. This powers and programs the ESP32. Do not wire external +5V into its VIN/5V, 3V3, GPIO or USB supply.

5V brick → servo + motor only

Regulated 5V USB-A brick (preferably 2A) → USB-A male screw-terminal adapter → W01 and W02 → breadboard. Use only the marked +5V/VCC/VBUS and GND terminals. D+ and D− stay empty; D− is not ground.

2. Use these exact breadboard holes

Orient the board so row numbers increase downward and A–E are on the left of the centre channel. Only the first eight numbered rows are shown. Use the A–E side only; F–J, the remaining rows and the long edge power rails are unused. A1–E1 connect to each other, but row 1 does not connect to row 3. E and F do not connect across the centre gap.

Four independent strips: row 1 = +5V; row 3 = common ground; row 5 = servo signal; row 7 = motor control. Confirm these five-hole groups on your actual board with power disconnected. If markings differ, identify and label the equivalent strips before wiring.

3. Which connector goes where?

F–M: ESP32 and motor module · six leads

Board's male headerBreadboard hole

The jumper's female socket covers the component's metal male pin. Its male pin enters the named breadboard hole. Applies to W03–W05 and W09–W11. The boards need their headers already fitted. If your BOJACK kit contains only M–M leads, it does not supply these six F–M leads.

M–M: SG90 and adapter · five leads

Servo's female socketBreadboard hole

For W06–W08, insert a male jumper into the servo socket cavity corresponding to its coloured lead; the other male goes to the breadboard. For W01/W02, securely clamp one suitable solid-ended lead in the adapter and insert the other end into A1/A3. Do not cut the servo cable.

4. Confirm the complete paths

  • Power: adapter +5V → W01 → row 1 → W06 → servo red; row 1 → W09 → module VCC.
  • Ground: adapter GND → W02 → row 3 → W03 → ESP32 GND; the same strip connects W07 servo ground and W10 motor ground.
  • Servo control: ESP32 GPIO18 → W04 → A5/B5 connected strip → W08 → servo orange/yellow.
  • Motor control, conditional: ESP32 GPIO19 → W05 → A7/B7 connected strip → W11 → module IN/S.
W11 remains disconnected until input compatibility is established. The motor's 5V supply rating does not specify its control-input voltage. Confirm the actual module accepts 3.3V GPIO control with a 5V supply and cannot expose GPIO19 to an incompatible voltage. A low IN reading alone is insufficient. If the circuit/seller specification is unresolved, leave W11 out and W09 unplugged for servo-only tests. Detailed input check.

5. Power-up and assembly sequence

  1. With both sources unplugged, identify and label the four strips, then fit W01/W02 only. Leave component leads disconnected for the supply check. Verify the four strips, polarity and that no pin/strand bridges adjacent holes. Keep the brick and adapter supported on the bench.
  2. For the first supply check, leave loads disconnected: connect only W01/W02 to the breadboard and verify about +5V from row 1 to row 3 with a meter. Unplug again, then add W03–W08 and W10. Leave W09 (module VCC) unplugged for the initial servo-only test; add it for a supervised module check, since an unconnected IN may float. Never probe mains-side contacts; this guide covers the brick's USB output only.
  3. Attach the breadboard right outside wall and ESP32 left outside wall with insulating mounting tape; leave USB-C accessible toward the front. Route motor/servo cables outside the frame, with slack secured away from the gate, powder opening, clip tabs, scale and robot approach.
  4. Keep the servo horn removed for initial centering. Connect USB-C to the MacBook and flash/configure MicroPython using the existing software guide. The firmware holds motor PWM at zero and vibration disabled. Disconnect external power before fitting the horn/gate as described in the mechanical guide.
  5. After polarity/wiring checks, connect the external 5V supply for a supervised empty servo test. A servo may move at boot. Verify open/closed calibration without rubbing. Stop and unplug if there is a reset, jitter, hot connector or unexpected movement; inspect supply and contacts.
  6. Only after the actual motor input passes the documented compatibility check: unplug both sources, add W09 and W11, reconnect and explicitly enable vibration in the firmware setup page. Confirm Stop turns vibration off and closes/disarms the gate before adding a small flour charge.
