01 / One plate. Four pieces.

Print the cradle, hopper, gate and lid together on your Bambu Lab A1.

  1. Open the geometry-only 3MF in Bambu Studio; keep its orientation.
  2. 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 5 top/bottom layers, 20% gyroid, no supports.
  3. Remove brims and clear the gate, shaft and zip-tie holes.
Slicer estimate: 221 g PLA / 6 h 31 min. Check the slicer preview before printing.

02 / Turn the cradle over

The cradle prints upside down. Turn it over so the two long legs rest on the bench.

  1. The recessed rectangular pocket faces upward.
  2. Use a level, stable surface with room for a collection cup.
The hopper is a drop-in fit, not a latch. Two retaining bands are required later.

03 / Secure the servo

Mount the SG90 above the hopper flange, with its output shaft pointing downward.

  1. Rest its mounting ears on the two raised pads.
  2. Use two small zip ties through the rectangular slots. Keep the shaft hole clear.
  3. Leave the horn off until the servo is electronically centered.
SG90 clone dimensions vary. Use thin card shims under the ears if needed; do not force the fit.

04 / Attach the vibration module

Tape the existing 5V PWM vibration module firmly to the outside of the hopper.

  1. Keep the motor and board outside the powder path.
  2. Use thin firm tape on the board back; insulate exposed solder joints.
  3. Face its connector outward so the lead cannot enter the moving gate.
A coin motor may be too weak for a full hopper. Start with a small amount of flour; flow is untested.

05 / Place the electronics

Use the 400-point breadboard on the front outer leg, and the ESP32 on the opposite outer leg.

  1. Tape the breadboard horizontally, centered on the flat leg below the flange.
  2. Use insulating foam tape behind the ESP32; keep its USB-C socket accessible sideways.
  3. Keep both retaining-band paths clear near the two ends of the cradle.
The ESP32 does not need to straddle the breadboard. Jumper leads connect its header pins to the junction board.

06 / Prepare the USB power lead

Use your 5V USB-A brick and a spare cut USB-A cable. The breadboard buses distribute power to the servo and motor.

  1. Unplug before cutting. Keep the USB-A plug; strip the other end. Verify +5V and GND with a meter—do not rely on wire colors alone.
  2. Join those two conductors securely to solid breadboard jumpers using connectors you own or solder plus insulation. Insulate each unused conductor separately.
  3. Verified +5V → positive bus; GND → ground bus and ESP32 GND. The MacBook USB-C cable separately powers/programs the ESP32.

No terminal adapter is needed. The cut lead requires secure insulated joints; loose strands or twist-and-tape joints are unsuitable. Use a 5V brick, preferably rated 2A. Never feed external +5V into an ESP32 GPIO or 3V3 pin.

07 / Wire the vibration module

Its motor uses 5V power; GPIO19 supplies only the 3.3V PWM control signal.

  1. VCC → +5V bus. GND → ground bus.
  2. IN/S → GPIO19 only after confirming 3.3V input compatibility.
  3. Before connecting IN to the ESP32, power the module alone and check IN-to-GND. A near-5V reading needs investigation first.

Similar modules accept 3.3V logic, but the exact Amazon board is undocumented. If compatibility is uncertain, leave IN disconnected and motor control disabled. No buffer chip is included in this build.

08 / Wire the servo

Connect the SG90 to the power junction and GPIO18.

  1. Red → +5V. Brown/black → common ground.
  2. Orange/yellow → GPIO18.
  3. Use male jumpers into the servo socket and female ends on ESP32 headers. Check that your kit includes the needed ends.

Do not power the servo from an ESP32 GPIO or its 3V3 pin. Route every cable outside the gate sweep.

09 / Install MicroPython

Use Python on your computer to flash the classic ESP32 and copy the controller. Arduino IDE is not needed.

  1. Disconnect external motor/servo power. Use a USB-C data cable.
  2. Install scripts/requirements.txt and run scripts/setup_device.py using your COM port and downloaded firmware.
  3. Read docs/SOFTWARE.md for the exact commands and ESP32 model check.
Flashing erases the ESP32. Use ESP32_GENERIC firmware only for a classic ESP32-WROOM board, not a C3/S3.

10 / Center before fitting the horn

Reconnect the external 5V supply, then center the servo with the gate and horn still removed.

  1. Connect your computer/phone to FlourProto and open 192.168.4.1.
  2. Press Center with horn removed. The nominal closed pulse is 1500 µs.
  3. Disconnect external power before installing the horn.
The device starts disarmed with vibration disabled. The public prototype Wi-Fi password is FlourTest26.

11 / Put the horn into the gate

Use the SG90 double-arm horn supplied with your servo.

  1. Seat the horn in the gate recess, spline socket facing up toward the servo.
  2. Secure it with two small zip ties through the paired slots.
  3. Put tie heads in the underside reliefs. Trim the tails flush.
If your horn differs from the recess, trim a spare horn or adjust the CAD. Do not stress the printed gate.

12 / Fit the gate and one screw

Fit the horn to the centered servo shaft with the gate hole turned away from the hopper outlet.

  1. The animation uses a nominal 60° closed position and a 0° open position.
  2. Install only the supplied servo horn screw from beneath the gate.
  3. Adjust card shims under the servo ears for roughly 0.4–0.8 mm clearance.
Turn by small calibration changes, never force the shaft. The gate must move freely; this is not a powder-tight seal.

13 / Seat the hopper

Lower the hopper flange into the cradle pocket.

  1. Check that all four edges sit flat on the ledge.
  2. Guide the wires outside the cradle and away from the gate.
  3. Place a cup beneath the outlet.
The pocket positions the hopper; it does not retain it against vibration or lifting.

14 / Add both retaining bands

Loop two narrow rubber bands around the complete cradle and hopper flange.

  1. One loop near each end: CAD X = −25 mm and +85 mm.
  2. Use bands at most 6 mm wide, clear of the rotating gate and electronics.
  3. Add small packing pads if needed so the bands actually clamp the recessed flange.
Before power: tug gently in every direction. If the hopper can lift or rock, improve the retention. Band strength and fit require physical testing.

15 / Dress the cables

Finish mounting the breadboard and ESP32 on their outer legs, with strain relief for the cables.

  1. Support the USB-A brick and insulated cut-cable joints on the bench, with strain relief.
  2. Give the hopper leads a small service loop, then secure them to the outside.
  3. Verify the full gate sweep clears both bands, all wires and the collection cup.
No additional printed electronics tray or mounting screws are needed. Foam tape must prevent the exposed ESP32 pins touching the frame or other conductors.

16 / Calibrate empty, then add flour

Test the gate empty before a small flour trial.

  1. Motor control stays disabled until you verify the module input, then explicitly enable it in the setup page.
  2. Set closed/open pulse widths in small increments, staying clear of mechanical stops.
  3. Start with one teaspoon of flour and a short, low-duty dose. Add the lid.
A full-duty startup pulse can help at low PWM settings; its supply remains 5V. Output mass, bridging and leakage are not yet measured.

17 / First controlled dose

Arm the controller and request a short timed dose. The cradle is shown transparent to reveal the gate. Stop closes it and stops vibration.

  1. Wi-Fi browser or Python client can arm, dose and stop.
  2. The controller limits a dose to 5 seconds and automatically disarms after 60 seconds.
  3. Disconnect power before touching or adjusting the mechanism.
Animation is illustrative. Gravity flow, vibration strength, retention and power-loss shutoff have not been physically validated.
