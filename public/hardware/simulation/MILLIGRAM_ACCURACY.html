Larger doses, ±10 mg.
A target to test, not a validated specification.

The current open-loop servo gate, vibration module and unconnected pocket scale have not demonstrated ±10 mg accuracy. The new 3D simulation checks rigid fit and motion. It does not calculate powder mass, bridging, discharge lag or accuracy.

Does the outlet need to be 14 mm?

No measured flour-flow study established 14 mm as the minimum or best diameter. It was a coarse prototype opening. Reducing an opening can limit flow but can also encourage intermittent blockage: cohesive powder can form a bridge even with a physically open outlet. The correct opening depends on the actual powder and conditions, not CAD alone. Jenike & Johanson: hopper bridging.

Keep the current print geometry for the fit study. First explore partial opening of the existing gate experimentally. Do not assume a newly printed tiny outlet will deliver ±10 mg. A removable restrictor or separate fine-feed head is a possible later revision after flow trials, not a validated part provided here.

What the simulation can calculate

Both outlet circles have radius 7 mm; the shaft offset is 35 mm. At relative gate angle θ from the fully aligned position, their centre separation is d = 70 |sin(θ/2)| mm. Their overlap is zero once d ≥ 14 mm, at about 23.1°. Thus the nominal 60° closed pose is already beyond complete geometric closure. The viewer shows overlap area, not a powder flow rate. Relative gate angle is not the raw servo command; calibration sets that mapping.

The scale matters

±10 mg is ±0.010 g. If your scale displays 0.01 g, one display increment is 10 mg. That does not establish ±10 mg accuracy: repeatability, calibration, drift and settling also matter. Readability and accuracy are distinct. METTLER TOLEDO: readability, precision and accuracy.

A stable, calibrated balance with 0.001 g (1 mg) readability and suitable repeatability would be a more useful starting point for testing this tolerance; that is an engineering selection recommendation, not an accuracy guarantee. Automatic correction also requires a supported USB/serial or other data interface. The current pocket scale has no connection to the ESP32. Begin with manual weight recording if you keep it, but do not claim it independently verifies ±10 mg.

Practical route with the current servo

  1. Calibrate fully closed and aligned-open poses. Confirm closure without rubbing. Use a small flour charge and record actual initial fill level.
  2. Characterize mechanically achievable short opening pulses and the mass delivered after each settles. Include variation at several hopper fill levels. An electrical pulse duration alone is not the actual gate-open duration.
  3. For a larger target, dispense coarsely, stopping with a margin greater than the largest observed fine-pulse delivery plus measurement uncertainty. Determine that margin from data rather than an arbitrary fixed percentage.
  4. Near target, apply a small calibrated pulse, close the gate, stop vibration, wait for a stable scale reading, and weigh again. A closed-loop controller would repeat this based on balance data; current firmware is timed/manual and does not implement balance feedback.
  5. If the smallest repeatable increment or post-close spill exceeds the tolerance budget, software timing alone is insufficient. Investigate a fine vibratory trickle feed or small metering screw, then characterize it with the actual flour. No new fine-feed hardware is included in the current build.
  6. To evaluate a candidate setting, record at least 30 independent doses per selected target and repeat at multiple fill levels. Record target, actual stable mass, signed error, repeatability and late dribble. Judge the ±10 mg band using a reference balance capable of resolving the error. Passing those trials supports only the tested conditions.

Commercial fine powder systems use gravimetric feedback rather than assuming that gate position equals delivered mass. Chemspeed: gravimetric powder dispensing. Their performance does not transfer to this budget prototype.

Current decision

The assembled reference geometry has no detected unintended solid overlap in the documented sweep. Mechanical fit is conditional on the stated horn assumptions. Powder dispensing at ±10 mg remains an experimental objective; neither this simulation nor the 14 mm hole establishes it. The earlier 1 mg total-dose/±1 mg discussion is superseded by the user's larger-dose ±10 mg target.
