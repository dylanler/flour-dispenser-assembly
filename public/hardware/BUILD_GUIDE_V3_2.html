Parts and printing

The repair plate contains six pieces: hopper, gate, three optional packing rings (0.4 / 0.8 / 1.6 mm), and a 0.6 mm strip that is removed before operation. A1 stock 0.4 mm nozzle, matching PETG preset, 0.20 mm Standard, 3 walls, 5 top/bottom layers, 15% gyroid, supports off. Keep supplied orientation; the 3MF contains geometry/layout only. The earlier 86.64 g / 3 h 22 min estimate was sliced at 20%; your 15% estimate will differ.

STEP 11 / 17

Seat the supplied horn first

Fit the SG90’s supplied double-arm horn directly onto the centered shaft, below the hopper.

Rendered from the actual V3.2 print meshes. Horn/socket and electronics are reference illustrations, physically unverified. Open this animated step ↗

  1. Fully seat its spline socket without forcing it. The horn never passes through the hopper opening.
  2. Gently secure the horn with its supplied centre screw. Do not use the screw to pull a partly seated or interfering horn into place.

The pictured horn/socket is a reference with assumed dimensions, not proof of the purchased unit’s seating. Do not trim the horn or alter its spline to match this illustration.
STEP 12 / 17

Fit the new gate and optional packing

Exploded alignment before lifting: the horn is already seated on the shaft. Packing pieces beside the head are optional and not installed in this reference.

Lift the V3.2 gate around the already seated horn, then secure the horn arms to its pocket floor.

Rendered from the actual V3.2 print meshes. Horn/socket and electronics are reference illustrations, physically unverified. Open this animated step ↗

  1. Start with no packing. Put the arms in the long pocket, with their flat outer faces toward the floor; align a closed gate position before fastening.
  2. Aim for a free 0.4–0.8 mm gate gap. If it rubs, remove the gate and add the smallest needed combination of 0.4 / 0.8 / 1.6 mm packing under the arms. Packing lowers the gate; it is not a servo-ear spacer.
  3. Use two ties in the gate’s upper/lower cross-grooves to clamp the arms and any packing. Tighten evenly without bowing the gate; keep the horn fully seated.
The 0.6 mm strip is a removable reference aid, not a required installed part or pass/fail gauge: a valid 0.5 mm gap will not admit it. Do not add packing merely to force the strip in. Remove it before movement.

STEP 16 / 17

Calibrate empty, then fit the lid

Check the assembled gate using small powered calibration movements before adding flour.

Rendered from the actual V3.2 print meshes. Horn/socket and electronics are reference illustrations, physically unverified. Open this animated step ↗

  1. Verify no rubbing, trapped tie, stalled servo or unseated horn. Set actual open/closed limits in the controller.
  2. CAD uses 0° aligned-open and 60° nominal closed; the 14 mm holes already stop overlapping near 23.1° relative rotation.
  3. Only after empty checks, add a small flour charge and the retained lid. Enable vibration only after its input check.
This is a clearance gate, not a powder-tight seal or mechanically fail-closed valve. Never force the shaft by hand or use more servo travel to overcome binding.
STEP 17 / 17

Run a short dose and weigh

Arm a short supervised dose, then close the gate and let the scale settle.

Rendered from the actual V3.2 print meshes. Horn/socket and electronics are reference illustrations, physically unverified. Open this animated step ↗

  1. Start small. The controller limits a dose to 5 seconds and disarms after 60 seconds.
  2. Stop vibration before reading the scale. It has no electronic connection to the ESP32; dosing remains timed.
  3. Record actual delivery before pursuing larger doses with ±10 mg accuracy. The visual motion is not a powder-flow simulation.
Neither a 0.01 g display increment nor the animation proves ±10 mg accuracy. Flour bridging, late dribble, supply performance and real robot/cup pickup remain untested.
